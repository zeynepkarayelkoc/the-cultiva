A dark background, a young woman glancing over her shoulder, a blue and yellow turban and a large pearl catching the light. Girl with a Pearl Earring is the kind of image people remember after a single look. Yet much of what we think we know about it is wrong or incomplete. She is not a portrait. The pearl is probably not a pearl. And the background was not always black. Here is what the painting really is, and why it still holds us.

The painting at a glance

  • Artist: Johannes Vermeer (1632-1675), Delft.
  • Date: about 1665.
  • Medium: oil on canvas, about 44.5 x 39 cm, much smaller than most visitors expect.
  • Where to see it: the Mauritshuis in The Hague, the Netherlands.
  • Earlier title: until 1995 it was usually called Girl with a Turban.

Who was the girl? Not a portrait, but a tronie

This is the question everyone asks, and the answer is surprising: probably no one in particular. Art historians classify the painting as a tronie, a popular type in seventeenth-century Dutch painting. A tronie is a study of a head, an expression or an exotic costume, not a commissioned likeness of a named sitter. The turban is part of that. It was not everyday Dutch clothing but a costume, a piece of imagined "Eastern" dress.

That has not stopped people guessing. Vermeer's eldest daughter Maria and a household servant are among the candidates suggested over the years. No document supports any of them. Tracy Chevalier's 1999 novel and the 2003 film with Scarlett Johansson imagined the girl as a maid called Griet. It is fiction, but much of the painting's modern fame comes from it.

Is the pearl real?

Probably not. A natural pearl of that size would have been almost impossibly rare and expensive in the 1660s. In 2014 the astrophysicist Vincent Icke argued that the way the earring reflects light does not match a real pearl and looks more like polished tin or glass. The Mauritshuis's own research shows the painting side of the trick: the earring is made of just two strokes of paint, a soft reflection below and a bright highlight above. There is not even a hook attaching it to her ear. Our eyes complete the rest.

What the Mauritshuis research found

In 2018 the painting was examined for two weeks in a glass studio inside the Mauritshuis, in full view of visitors, in a project called The Girl in the Spotlight. The results, announced in 2020, brought several surprises.

  • Eyelashes: the girl once had fine eyelashes and eyebrows, which have almost entirely faded.
  • A green curtain: the background was not originally plain black. Vermeer painted her in front of a dark green curtain, whose colour has darkened and disappeared over the centuries.
  • Precious blue: the deep blue of the turban is ultramarine, made from lapis lazuli mined in Afghanistan, one of the most expensive pigments of the time.

Sold for two guilders and thirty cents

Today the painting is considered priceless, but it was nearly forgotten. In 1881, in poor condition, it was bought at an auction in The Hague by the collector Arnoldus Andries des Tombe for just two guilders and thirty cents. When des Tombe died in 1902, he left it to the Mauritshuis in his will. As the museum puts it, they paid nothing for it, and today she is priceless.

What does the painting mean?

Most of Vermeer's paintings show women absorbed in quiet tasks in a room: pouring milk, reading a letter, weighing pearls. Girl with a Pearl Earring has no room, no task and no story. There is only a glance. She seems to have turned at the very moment someone said her name, lips slightly parted as if about to speak. That direct, unfinished moment of contact is the whole subject. It is why she is sometimes called the "Mona Lisa of the North", and why viewers keep projecting their own stories onto her.

Compare her with Vermeer's The Milkmaid in the Rijksmuseum. There, light falls through a window onto a calm everyday task. Here, light falls only on a face, a pearl and a pair of lips, and everything else stays in shadow. Two kinds of silence from the same painter.

FAQ

Who painted Girl with a Pearl Earring?

The Dutch painter Johannes Vermeer, around 1665.

Who was the girl with the pearl earring?

Probably no specific person. The painting is a tronie, a study of a head and costume, and the model is unknown.

Is the pearl in Girl with a Pearl Earring real?

Probably not. A pearl that large would have been extremely rare, and some researchers think it may be polished tin or glass. In the painting it is made of only two brushstrokes.

Where is Girl with a Pearl Earring?

In the Mauritshuis museum in The Hague, the Netherlands.
